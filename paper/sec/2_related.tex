\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Visual intermediate states.} Visual Sketchpad~\cite{hu2024sketchpad} lets a model draw on the image with tools and look again. Mirage~\cite{yang2026mirage} and Latent Visual Reasoning~\cite{li2025lvr} train models to emit latent visual tokens between text steps. MindCube~\cite{wang2026mindcube} finds that supplying unseen intermediate views, or having the model write a cognitive map first, improves spatial reasoning from limited views. MVoT~\cite{li2025mvot} trains a multimodal model to generate an image of the state after each reasoning step. We train nothing and instead ask what a perfect image would be worth: we measure what an exact intermediate image is worth on our tasks, test with interventions whether the intermediate image is causally used, and quantify how much error it can tolerate.

\paragraph{Spatial and physical reasoning in VLMs.} VLMs struggle with basic spatial relations~\cite{kamath2023whatsup} and with perceptual tasks humans solve at a glance~\cite{fu2024blink}. Recent work reports a strong egocentric bias in perspective taking, with most errors reproducing the camera's own view~\cite{wang2026egocentric}; we observe the same bias and find that an imagined view removes it when shown alone, and only partly when the original image is shown alongside. Our scenes follow the synthetic, fully controlled style of CLEVR~\cite{johnson2017clevr}, and our stability task follows the block-tower setting of Lerer \etal~\cite{lerer2016blocks}.
